This patio contained a number of interesting hurdles, read on if you’re interested!

-

The first step was to dig out a large square hole to give us space to install the base substrate of the patio - if you put pavers or tiles directly on the soil it’s just one rainy season away from squiffy!

We hauled out 7 massive concrete piles - the remnants of a bygone structure, and removed over 60 cubic feet of some seriously tough earth.

Once we had the area leveled (check with a line level) and evenly compacted we installed the outer pressure-treated lumber frame for the patio - and secured it with 8 rebar stakes, each driven two feet into the ground.

The frame not only gives us an easy way to flatten the layers of soil, gravel, and bedding sand, but it also acts as a barrier to keep the patio tiles from spreading out over time.

Next, Fynn and I added 3 inches of crushed aggregate - this allows water to easily drain and won’t expand or contract at different moisture levels, which would cause the patio to become uneven and squiffy over time. 

We compacted this gravel and screeded an inch of bedding sand using a jig we made - the bedding sand gives a nice even surface for the tiles to sink into while also allowing any rainfall to drain into the ground.

Finally we began placing the Rünnen tiles, Fynn was on placing duty while I was trimming and cutting the tiles on our table saw for a perfect fit!

To finish it off we compacted the tiles into the sand with the tamper, being careful to place a piece of cardboard between the tamper and tiles so as not to damage them.

Then we hosed it off and took some pictures!
